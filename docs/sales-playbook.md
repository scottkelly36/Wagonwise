# Sales playbook for the fleet portal

**Status:** a first draft to try and rewrite. It has three tools: a case study template for the pilot firm, a cold message and phone script, and a 20-minute demo script. Fill the `[brackets]` yourself. Nothing here has been tested on a real prospect, so change the wording as soon as a real conversation shows what lands.

**The offer in one line:** your jobs, vehicle checks, maintenance dates and what each job made, in one place, with a free driver app that connects to it. £10 per vehicle per month; the driver app is free. [Decide the trial terms: for example, the first month free, and we set your company up for you.]

Use this order: drivers like the app, the firm sees the portal, the firm pays. Write about one problem at a time, and sell the problem the firm already feels.

## 1. Case study template (for the pilot firm)

Ask the pilot firm if you can name them. If they would rather not, call it "a Northumberland haulier with [number] vehicles". Get their words, not yours: ask the questions, write down what they say, send it back for approval before you use it. Do not publish anything they have not approved.

**Questions to ask (15 minutes, in person or by phone)**

1. What did you use for jobs, checks and maintenance before? (Paper, WhatsApp, a spreadsheet, a system?)
2. What was the worst part of that?
3. What do you use WagonWise for now, week to week?
4. What has changed? (Time saved, problems caught, fewer phone calls, fewer arguments about a delivery.)
5. Give me one example from the last month. (A defect caught, a delivery proved, a late job spotted.)
6. What would you tell another haulier thinking about it?
7. Is there anything that still annoys you? (This is the most useful answer for what to build next.)

**One-page layout**

> **[Firm name], [town]: [one line in the firm's own words, for example "We stopped chasing drivers on the phone"]**
>
> **The firm:** [vehicles, drivers, what they haul, how long in business].
> **Before:** [two sentences from question 1 and 2].
> **With WagonWise:** [three bullets: what they use it for].
> **What changed:** [one real example, with a number if they have one].
> **In their words:** "[a short quote]", [name, role].
>
> WagonWise is a free driver app and a fleet portal for UK haulage firms. [Website and contact].

**Rules:** only claims the firm has said and approved; no invented numbers; a photo only if they choose to give one.

## 2. Cold message and phone script

**Who to contact:** the owner or transport manager, by name. Use your prospects workbook. Check the firm's own website or Companies House for a name.

**Email rules (a quick check, not legal advice):** you can generally email a limited company at a business address for business reasons. A sole trader or a partnership is treated more like an individual, so use a phone call or LinkedIn first and do not email without consent. Always give a clear way to say no, and keep a list of anyone who does. Check the ICO's current guidance on direct marketing before you start a run of messages.

**Short message (email or LinkedIn)**

> Subject: [first name], quick question about your walk-round checks
>
> Hi [first name],
>
> I run WagonWise, a free driver app and a fleet portal for UK hauliers. We are working with [pilot firm / a Northumberland haulier] on [one problem: keeping walk-round checks and MOT dates in one place, with defects sent straight to the office].
>
> Do you still do [checks on paper / jobs by WhatsApp]? If so, could I show you a 15-minute demo on your own jobs? No charge, and no obligation.
>
> [Name], [phone], [website]
>
> If you would rather I did not get in touch again, just say so and I will not.

Replace the bracketed problem with the one that fits that firm. One problem per message.

**Phone script (60 seconds)**

1. **Opening:** "Hi [first name], it is [name] from WagonWise. Is this a bad time? I will be quick."
2. **Why you are calling:** "I make a free driver app and a fleet portal for hauliers. I am working with [pilot firm] at the moment."
3. **The question:** "Can I ask how you keep track of [walk-round checks / maintenance dates / where the lorries are] at the moment?"
4. **Listen.** Write down their words. Ask "what is the worst part of that?"
5. **The offer:** "I can show you how we handle that in 15 minutes, using your own jobs. Would [day] or [day] suit?"
6. **If no:** "No problem. Can I send you a one-page summary in case it is useful later?" Then stop.

**Follow-up rule:** one reminder a week later, one after a month, then leave them alone unless they reply.

## 3. Demo script (20 minutes)

**Before the call:** ask for their vehicle count and the one problem they mentioned. Use the dummy-data company, so nothing real is on screen. Open the portal and the driver app side by side.

| Minutes  | What to show                 | What to say                                                                                                          |
| -------- | ---------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0 to 2   | Ask first                    | "Before I show you anything, tell me how you run jobs and checks today." Write down what they say.                   |
| 2 to 5   | Jobs                         | Create a job with two stops, assign a driver and a vehicle. "The driver is told at once and sees it in the app."     |
| 5 to 8   | The driver app               | Show the job on the phone: accept, stops, proof of delivery photo. "You see the status change as they go."           |
| 8 to 11  | Live trips                   | The map with vehicles on a job. "It only updates while the driver has the app open, so check last seen."             |
| 11 to 14 | Their problem                | Go to the page that fixes the problem they named: checks and defects, maintenance dates, or job profit.              |
| 14 to 16 | Job profit and looking ahead | "This is what each job cost and made." Only if they are interested in money; skip it otherwise.                      |
| 16 to 18 | Setting up                   | "I set your company up for you, import your vehicles and drivers, and stay on call for the first week."              |
| 18 to 20 | The close                    | State the price: "£10 per vehicle per month, [trial terms]. Shall we start with [a handful of vehicles / one week]?" |

**After the demo**

- Send a short email the same day: what you showed, the price, and the next step with a date.
- Offer a small start: two or three vehicles, not the whole fleet.
- If they say "not now", ask what would have to be true for it to be a yes, and write the answer down.

**Things to avoid:** a long feature tour; showing pages they did not ask about; promising features that are not built; showing any real firm's data.

## Keep a tally

Each week write down: messages sent, calls made, demos booked, demos given, trials started, trials paying. After a month you will see which step loses people, and that is the one to fix.
